What is missing from this clothing budget?

An item price is one input, not a complete delivered-cost record. Use this workbook to identify the questions you still need to answer. It records your own assessment of six categories; it does not look up prices, determine taxes, contact a provider or calculate an official bill.

Classify each category from its source

Select “not applicable” only when you have a reason for that conclusion. Otherwise retain “unknown.” An absent number is not evidence of a zero charge. Amounts and personal order details stay in your separate private worksheet.

No review yet. Begin with the sources you actually have.

Scope comes before a total

A comparison needs a selected quantity, destination, currency and included service scope. A parcel quotation for several items cannot be compared directly with the displayed starting price of one shirt. Record whether packaging, delivery options or optional services were included, rather than inferring their treatment from the total.

If you allocate one parcel charge across several items, label the allocation rule. An equal split is a budget model unless the provider actually issued separate item charges. When recombining item budgets, do not add those allocated shares and then add the whole parcel amount again.

Quotes and payments describe different evidence

A dated quote is an estimate for its stated scope. A payment record shows an amount paid at a particular stage. Keep both if the amount changes. The difference alone does not establish its cause, and a carrier scan cannot verify the price you paid.

For a missing fee, write a narrow question: does this quotation include that service for this destination and selected parcel? Confirm current terms through the provider's verified channel. This page offers no tax, legal or refund-entitlement advice.
